# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring managers, recruiters, prospective clients, BCG peers, and other strategists or product builders considering Kevin Lowe (BCG Principal, Gen AI Node Lead) for engagements, advisory, or collaboration. They arrive having seen a LinkedIn profile or referral and are deciding within 60–90 seconds whether to read deeper or reach out. They are on a laptop in a quiet office or on mobile between meetings.

## Product Purpose

A living resume that does what a PDF cannot: prove the claim. Kevin's pitch is "Strategist Who Builds" — a McKinsey-tier strategist who has crossed the chasm into shipping production code with agentic AI workflows. The site itself is the proof point. If the design feels like a built product (not a template, not a Squarespace), the thesis lands before the visitor reads a word. Success looks like: visitors stop scrolling LinkedIn, read the Philosophy section in full, and email kevin@wanderluxe.io within the same session.

## Brand Personality

Three words: confident, considered, warm. Voice is editorial — long-form sentences in dark serif, dropped capitals, numbered sections (01–05), a magazine masthead's discipline. Not corporate consulting, not VC-tech, not portfolio cliché. The emotional goal is the quiet authority of someone who has nothing to prove and is therefore allowed to be playful (a Le Cordon Bleu side quest, a 3D-printed drone, a video of a golf swing tucked under "Other"). The site should feel like a print magazine that happens to live on the web.

## Anti-references

- **Stripe / Linear / Vercel marketing sites** — the saturated dark-tech-product aesthetic. This site is not a SaaS pitch.
- **Squarespace / Notion templates** — generic portfolio scaffolding with stock imagery and identical card grids.
- **Consultant-corporate sites** (Bain, BCG official, McKinsey) — navy + gray + serif headings + safe.
- **Resume-as-card layouts** — the "personal site" cliché where every section is an identical bordered card with an icon + heading + paragraph.
- **Y-Combinator / dev portfolio dark mode** — neon-on-black, terminal aesthetic. The opposite of what we want.

## Design Principles

1. **The medium is the proof.** Every craft choice in this site (typography, motion, restraint) should silently demonstrate the thesis: "strategy and execution by the same brain." If the design feels considered, the bio is true.
2. **Print magazine, not portfolio site.** Borrow editorial discipline: numbered sections, generous whitespace, dropped capitals, serif body for moments of weight. Avoid every web cliché the print world doesn't have.
3. **Reveal, don't dump.** Surface the strategic story up top (Hero → How I Think → Experience). Tuck depth (project origin stories, role bullets, baking photos, golf video) behind progressive disclosure. The first scroll is a poster; the second scroll is the book.
4. **Warm restraint.** One paper palette, one ink, one warm accent. No second accent. No gradients on text. Color earns its place by being almost the only color.
5. **Show range without scattering.** The breadth claim ("strategy → code → pastry → drone") is core to the thesis but easy to mishandle as noise. Cluster, sequence, and gate breadth carefully so it reads as range, not randomness.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target: WCAG 2.1 AA on color contrast, keyboard navigation, and reduced motion. The site already honors `prefers-reduced-motion` via a global CSS rule and the `AnimationContext`. Known gaps to address: visible focus indicators on every interactive element (currently keyboard focus rings are inconsistent), and a real mobile navigation (the current mobile nav collapses to a single "Contact" link with no section navigation).
