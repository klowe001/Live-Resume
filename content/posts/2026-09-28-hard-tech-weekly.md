## Regulatory changes

### BIS: polysilicon stockpiling caps
Temporary final rule published Sept 24, effective Sept 22 to Dec 3. Commerce monitors incumbent importers for abnormal volumes above historic average and caps any importer that registered with CBP on or after Aug 6, 2026 at 12 kg/week under HTS 2804.61.00, 7 kg/week across the 3818.00.00 derivative codes, 2,000 units/week under 8541.42.00 and 55 units/week under 8541.43.00. Issued without notice and comment on national security grounds. Hits solar manufacturers, wafer buyers and anyone newly sourcing polysilicon into the US ahead of the Dec 4 tariff date. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/24/2026-19537/measures-to-restrict-stockpiling-of-polysilicon-and-polysilicon-derivatives-under-proclamation-11052))

### NRC: wholesale reactor regulation overhaul
Proposed rule published Sept 24, docket NRC-2025-1138, touching 10 CFR Parts 20, 21, 50, 52, 53, 55, 70, 72 and 75. Eliminates six nonemergency event reporting categories, expands partial site release and shortens waiting periods before major decommissioning activities, conforms Part 50 and 52 financial qualification to Part 53, and moves seismic design and PRA requirements to a risk-informed basis. Comments close **Nov 9, 2026 at 11:59pm ET**. Affects every reactor developer, decommissioning operator and component supplier under Part 21. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/24/2026-19568/regulatory-enhancements-for-reactor-licensing-decommissioning-and-operational-oversight))

### State Department: ITAR policies of denial and MNNA list
The Sept 18 rule (91 FR 59059) clarified policy-of-denial provisions, revised country policies for Ethiopia and Somalia, and added **Saudi Arabia and Peru** to the Major Non-NATO Ally list. A correcting amendment effective Sept 23 restored text inadvertently deleted from §125.4(b)(10)(i)-(iii) on unclassified technical data releases by US educational institutions and §126.1(a)(2)-(3) on permitted USG transfers. MNNA status eases license review for defense exports to those two markets. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/23/2026-19404/international-traffic-in-arms-regulations-clarifying-policies-of-denial-updating-the-major-non-nato))

### FAA: Relativity Space Terran R NEPA clearance
Finding of No Significant Impact and Record of Decision published Sept 28 for the Terran R launch program at Cape Canaveral SFS, which unblocks the licensing path for the vehicle at LC-16. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/28/2026-19716/notice-of-availability-on-the-finding-of-no-significant-impact-and-record-of-decision-for-relativity))

### Section 232 robotics and industrial machinery: still pending, past deadline
The investigation covering CNC machining centers, grinding and stamping equipment, automatic tool changers, EDM and laser/waterjet cutting (UAS expressly excluded) was initiated Sept 2, 2025 with comments closed Oct 17, 2025. The statutory 270-day Commerce report deadline passed around May 30, 2026 and the President's 90-day decision window around late August. No report, determination or proclamation has been published as of Sept 28. This is the largest unpriced tariff risk for anyone importing industrial automation equipment, and it would arrive as a proclamation with little warning. ([Docket](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/09/26/2025-18749/notice-of-request-for-public-comments-on-section-232-national-security-investigation-of-imports-of))

### Italy: nuclear framework restored
The Senate voted 81–51 with 7 abstentions on Sept 24 to approve a return to nuclear power, following the Chamber's 155–86 vote in June. The law delegates the government to create a National Programme for Sustainable Nuclear Power and an independent Nuclear Safety Authority, Italy's first nuclear framework in roughly 40 years. ([World Nuclear News](https://world-nuclear-news.org/articles/italys-senate-approves-a-return-to-nuclear-power))

## Robotics

IFR forecasts 655,000 installs in 2026 and 806,000 by 2029. The China-versus-everyone-else gap is now roughly nine to one against the US. Advanced materials and biomanufacturing were also quiet, with Eli Lilly's $6.5B Houston API groundbreaking on Sep 21 the only substantive item in the latter.
