Atoms, not bits. Week of October 5 to 12, 2026.

## Top of the week

- Schneider Electric is buying **PTC** for $205.00/share, $22.6B equity and $23.7B enterprise value, the largest industrial software deal on record, taking the electrical-infrastructure incumbent straight into the PLM/CAD stack against Siemens; close expected Q3 2027 ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/857005/000119312526413124/d174191dex991.htm))
- The FAA dropped **five separate Part 450 NPRMs on one day**, all under EO 14335, all with comments due Nov 4, cutting launch and reentry licensing burden on blast analysis, lightning mitigation for booster descent, flight safety analysis, physical containment and paper filing ([example NPRM](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20391/lightning-hazard-mitigation-burden-reducing-exception))
- Nuclear's mechanism of the week was uprates, not new build: Google and Constellation signed a 20-year PPA for **890 MW** of new capacity across 11 reactors with over $4.3B of Constellation investment ([Google](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-10-06-Google-and-Constellation-Announce-Landmark-Agreement-to-Bring-890-MW-of-New-Nuclear-Capacity-to-PJM-Grid-as-Part-of-Long-Term-Power-Deal)), days after DOE conditionally committed up to $4.2B to Vistra for 433 MW of uprates ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-department-announces-42-billion-investment-boost-nuclear-power-and-help-lower))
- Interceptor production rate was the defense theme: Boeing locked a roughly **$14.7B** undefinitized seven-year agreement to triple PAC-3 MSE seeker output at Huntsville ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/industry/techwatch/2026/10/06/boeing-locks-in-7-year-deal-to-triple-pac-3-mse-seeker-output/)), alongside Lockheed's PAC-3 Edge reveal and Anduril's Steelfin, both claiming ~2,000 units/year as the target rate
- GlobalFoundries will make silicon interposers for TSMC's CoWoS under a **$2B**, five-year agreement at Malta, NY, the first US-based source for a real AI-accelerator packaging bottleneck, ramping 1H 2028 ([GF](https://investors.gf.com/news-releases/news-release-details/globalfoundries-reaches-agreement-establish-us-based-supply))

## Regulatory changes

Published or effective Oct 5 to 12.

### FAA: commercial space licensing, five NPRMs, Oct 5
All five amend 14 CFR Part 450 (plus Parts 401/404/413/414), all cite EO 14335 "Enabling Competition in the Commercial Space Industry," and **all close for comment Nov 4, 2026**. Affects every launch and reentry licensee.

- **Overpressure blast effects** (FAA-2026-9941, RIN 2120-AM31): deletes "hypothetical" from §450.137(c)(6) so far-field analysis covers only existing populated locations ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20392/overpressure-blast-effects-analysis-burden-reducing-clarification))
- **Lightning hazard exception** (FAA-2026-9940, RIN 2120-AM30): new §450.163(b) exempts descending flight where the path is physics-constrained to stay clear of public and critical assets, which explicitly covers first-stage and barge landings; codifies what was previously case-by-case ELOS ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20391/lightning-hazard-mitigation-burden-reducing-exception))
- **Flight safety analysis means of compliance** (FAA-2026-9936, RIN 2120-AM40): one submission for the whole FSA method instead of per sub-analysis ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20388/streamlining-flight-safety-analysis-methodology-means-of-compliance))
- **Digital filing** (FAA-2026-9935, RIN 2120-AM39): adds the planned LEAP portal and eliminates paper submissions ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20387/digital-modernization-for-licensing-electronic-application-submissions))
- **Physical containment** (FAA-2026-9934, RIN 2120-AM38): removes the flight-hazard-area obligation when containment is used ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20386/simplifying-physical-containment-requirements))

### CBP: de minimis overhaul NPRM, Oct 8
91 FR 64532, docket USCBP-2026-0298, RIN 1685-AA38, **comments due Dec 7, 2026**. Implements suspension and planned termination of the Section 321 de minimis exemption. Informal type 11 entries for goods at or under $2,500 must be filed electronically by the date of importation rather than within 15 days after arrival; a new electronic type 13 mail entry replaces the postal informal process; consignees who are not the owner or purchaser must use a licensed broker as importer of record; type 11/13 filers need a bond with a $1,000 liquidated-damages minimum. Relevant detail for hardware: **the $250 ceiling for certain Chapter 99 goods including Section 232 goods is removed**, allowing informal entry up to $2,500. Hits anyone importing components, prototypes or spares in small parcels. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/08/2026-20650/low-value-shipments))

### CBP: IEEPA tariff refund mechanism advances, Oct 5
CBP sought OMB renewal of the worksheet behind its CAPE refund tool (OMB 1651-0149, docket USCBP-2026-0826, comments due Nov 4). The notice documents the posture: following *Learning Resources v. Trump* (Feb 20, 2026) holding IEEPA tariffs unlawful, CBP faces roughly **$166B in refundable duties across more than 53 million entry summaries** for entries from Feb 3, 2025 to Feb 24, 2026, paid with interest; filers submit CSVs via the ACE Portal, up to 9,999 entries per declaration. The CIT has suspended immediate-compliance pressure while CAPE is built and may lift that at any time. Large working-capital item for any importer of record. ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20228/agency-information-collection-activities-court-ordered-refunds-under-the-international-emergency))

### USTR: Section 301 China actions continued, Oct 7
USTR determined the July 6, 2018 and Aug 23, 2018 actions did not terminate on their four-year anniversaries and remain in effect, on the strength of continuation requests from 68 producers plus 18 associations, and 57 plus 19, respectively. The statutory Section 307(c)(3) review follows, with a separate notice to come. The only operative effect is continuation; no change to product coverage, rates or exclusions, and the notice does not restate rates ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/07/2026-20510/continuation-of-actions-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to-technology-transfer)). A separate conforming amendment preserved an exclusion's coverage under new stat reporting number 8479.90.9591, effective retroactively to July 1, 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/07/2026-20511/notice-of-conforming-amendment-to-product-exclusion-chinas-acts-policies-and-practices-related-to)).

### NRC: Clinch River permit published, materials licensing relaxed
**Clinch River Unit 1**: Construction Permit CPAR-2 to TVA plus Record of Decision published Oct 5 (Docket 50-615, 91 FR 63311), immediately effective. Date nuance worth keeping straight: the permit itself issued Sept 28, outside the window; Oct 5 is the FR publication. The notice does not name the reactor design ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20345/tennessee-valley-authority-clinch-river-nuclear-unit-1-construction-permit-and-record-of-decision)).

**Exemptions From Materials Licensing** (NRC-2025-1568, RIN 3150-AL61, 91 FR 63133): direct final rule plus companion proposed rule, Oct 5. Replaces the Schedule B table in 10 CFR 30.71 with a cross-reference to appendix C of Part 20, adding radionuclides used in research, industrial and medical settings, and extends standing exemptions in 30.12, 40.11 and 70.11 to temporary law-enforcement and emergency-responder training below set quantities. **Effective Dec 21, 2026 unless significant adverse comments arrive by Nov 4** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/05/2026-20336/exemptions-from-materials-licensing)).

**Palisades**: NRC is reviewing a Holtec license amendment request to use custom equipment and procedures to move a damaged, tilted fuel assembly from the core to the spent fuel pool, with a comment opportunity open. The release states no deadline ([NRC](https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/news-releases/2026/nrc-reviewing-palisades-license-amendment-request-public-comment-opportunity-open)).

## Robotics

Thin week on substance. The most technically interesting item was Boston Dynamics detailing its [redesigned Atlas hand](https://www.therobotreport.com/boston-dynamics-gives-more-insight-into-its-redesigned-humanoid-hand/): four fingers, 13 DoF, driven by 13 *identical* fully enclosed actuators with no cables crossing joints, explicitly for mass manufacturing and for sim fidelity so RL transfers. Strength parity with earlier hands that lifted 100+ lb. They dropped the pinky, shrank the gripper, and rejected a two-thumb concept on actuation cost and volume. Not yet fleet-wide, and testing at the Hyundai Metaplant site has not started.

Helm.ai disclosed [$70M in signed commercial contracts](https://www.therobotreport.com/helm-ai-reaches-70m-signed-commercial-contracts-foundation-models/) and an expansion from automotive into industrial automation, a rare contracted-revenue datapoint in a sector that discloses almost nothing. FireDome launched US operations with ground-based [wildfire-suppression artillery](https://www.therobotreport.com/firedome-builds-autonomous-artillery-for-wildfire-defense/), ~10-gallon capsules to ~1,000 ft covering ~70 acres per unit, at $10K to $15K/month as a service, first deployments spring 2027. KARI's fifth Nuri flight (Oct 7) carried 15 payloads and deployed 14; [PERSAT-02 failed to release](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/tech-science/20261007/nuri-rocket-deploys-14-satellites-1-cubesat-fails-to-release), a near-miss on Korea's most complex Nuri mission. Falcon 9 flew [SDA Tranche 1 Transport Layer A](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/spacex-falcon-9-rocket-launch-data-transport-satellites-military-proliferated-architecture) from Vandenberg (Oct 10) with 21 Northrop-built satellites, booster B1103 on its sixth flight, after an in-final-minute abort Oct 5 and slips on the 6th and 8th. That is the fourth of nine SpaceX launches for Tranche 1, bringing 84 of 126 transport satellites up with 42 to go.

**NASA and DOE signed a space nuclear MOU** (Oct 8, RELEASE26-084) covering research, fuel production, testing, launch integration and operations, effective Nov 1. Named programs: Space Reactor-1 Freedom for propulsion targeting a 2028 launch, Lunar Reactor-1 for fission surface power through lunar night, Dragonfly's MMRTG with 24 light-weight radioisotope heater units, and 24 similar units for ESA's Rosalind Franklin rover. A December 2025 EO directs a launch-ready lunar surface reactor by 2030. No power-output figures appear in the release, so treat any kilowatt numbers elsewhere as not from this document ([NASA](https://nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-energy-department-advance-new-era-of-nuclear-powered-exploration)).

ESA and Vast signed a [post-ISS cooperation MoU](https://www.esa.int/Newsroom/Press_Releases/The_European_Space_Agency_and_Vast_Build_on_Existing_Mission_Collaborations_and_Strengthen_Cooperation_on_Post-ISS) at IAC (Oct 5): non-binding, initial three-year term, starting with a joint feasibility study, covering potential ESA astronaut missions to Haven, ESA hardware contributions, science accommodation, up and down mass, and European cargo and crew vehicles that could service Haven. This is the live story of European hedging against losing assured crewed access. Crew-12 splashed down Oct 8 after ~237 days. Sierra Space dropped its [Ghost](https://satnews.com/2026/10/11/sierra-space-conducts-high-altitude-drop-test-of-ghost-reentry-vehicle-for-90-minute-global-point-delivery/) reentry vehicle from 68,500 ft at Dugway under Space Command's Apollo Nexus exercise, with automated heat-shield jettison then steerable parafoil, landing within 100 m of target on a 10-ton article.

**Quiet:** no in-orbit servicing news, no major space contract awards, and ISRO and JAXA were silent.

## Semis, energy and other hard tech

Beyond the GF and TSMC interposer agreement above, Musk [ruled out any TSMC operational role](https://qz.com/elon-musk-terafab-tsmc-build-run-100726) in the Texas Terafab, saying Tesla and SpaceX will build and run it themselves and TSMC could at most sublease part of the site. Intel remains the only named chipmaking partner. Korea Economic Daily [reported](https://www.kedglobal.com/korean-chipmakers/newsView/ked202610070018) Samsung cleared customer qualification on 12-layer HBM4E with Nvidia and unnamed hyperscalers, citing people familiar; Samsung has not confirmed, and no bandwidth or mass-production timing was given.

Fusion had two real milestones. General Fusion and UKAEA measured **1.1 keV (12.6 million °C) electron temperatures on LM26** just before peak compression, claimed as the first fusion machine to reach 1 keV with low-speed compression, compressing over milliseconds rather than microseconds or nanoseconds. Validated with a Thomson scattering system co-designed with UKAEA, which built the polychromator at Culham; paper submitted for peer review, next target 10 keV ([General Fusion](https://generalfusion.com/post/general-fusion-ukaea-announce-magnetized-target-fusion-milestone/)). Separately, Europe delivered ITER's [fifth and final vacuum vessel sector](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/europe-delivers-final-iter-vacuum-vessel-sector), the ninth of nine and the last large component to reach Cadarache, so every major core-machine component is now on site. Each sector is over 14 m tall and 440 tonnes. Note that sources disagree on the schedule: World Nuclear News cites D-D operation in 2035 while ANS lists research operations 2034 and D-T 2039, so attribute whichever you use.

In fission, Prodigy selected BWXT's [BANR microreactor](https://investors.bwxt.com/news-releases/news-release-details/bwxts-banr-microreactor-chosen-canadian-transportable-nuclear) (75 MWt / 22 MWe) for the Indigenous-owned Belledune transportable nuclear plant pilot in New Brunswick with the Pabineau and Eel River Bar First Nations, installation targeted at about 18 months and in-service early 2030s, value undisclosed and a definitive agreement still pending. DOE will also award up to $50M over three years for silicon carbide fuel cladding, applications due Dec 7.

**Quiet:** no battery or grid-storage round closed and no US gigafactory or cell-chemistry announcement landed; the battery news this week was the SK On layoffs. Novel materials and biomanufacturing were otherwise empty apart from two *Nature* thorium-229 nuclear clock papers.
